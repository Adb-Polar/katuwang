## Music Theory & Appreciation
How many lines are in a standard musical staff?|Five.|Four.|Six.|Seven.|Standard staff notation uses five lines.
What primarily indicates how high or low a sound is?|Pitch.|Tempo.|Dynamics.|Duration.|Pitch describes perceived highness or lowness.
What does tempo describe?|The speed of the beat.|Loudness alone.|Instrument size.|The key signature only.|Tempo concerns the pace of beats.
What do dynamics describe?|Relative loudness and softness.|Note names only.|Staff line count.|The composer's age.|Dynamics shape musical intensity.
Which symbol indicates silence for a specified duration?|Rest.|Clef.|Sharp.|Bar line only.|Rests indicate measured silence.
What is timbre?|Tone color or sound quality.|Only beat speed.|Only note length.|The number of staff lines.|Timbre distinguishes sound qualities.
In common notation, what does a sharp usually do to a note?|Raises it by a semitone.|Lowers it by a semitone.|Doubles its duration.|Makes it silent.|A sharp raises pitch relative to the natural note.
What does a flat usually do?|Lowers a note by a semitone.|Raises a note by an octave.|Halves tempo.|Adds a rest.|A flat lowers the notated pitch.
In 4/4 time, a quarter note normally receives how many beats?|One.|Two.|Three.|Four.|The lower 4 assigns the beat to the quarter note.
In 4/4 time, how many quarter-note beats complete a measure?|Four.|Two.|Six.|Eight.|The upper number gives four beats per measure.
A passage gradually becomes louder. Which term fits?|Crescendo.|Decrescendo.|Ritardando.|Staccato.|Crescendo indicates increasing loudness.
A passage gradually becomes softer. Which term fits?|Decrescendo.|Crescendo.|Accelerando.|Fortissimo.|Decrescendo indicates decreasing loudness.
The treble clef identifies which line as G?|Second line from the bottom.|Bottom line as C.|Top line as A.|Middle line as B-flat always.|The G clef locates G on the second staff line.
A singer performs one unaccompanied melodic line. What texture is this?|Monophonic.|Polyphonic necessarily.|Atonal necessarily.|Percussive only.|Monophony contains one melodic line.
Two independent melodies sound together. What texture is emphasized?|Polyphonic.|Monophonic.|Silence.|Unpitched speech only.|Polyphony combines independent melodic lines.
A familiar melody returns after a contrasting section. Which form fits A–B–A?|Ternary.|Binary only.|A single repeated A section.|A random sequence without return.|Ternary form returns to an opening section after contrast.
Two instruments play the same pitch and loudness but sound different. What best explains it?|Different timbres.|Different staff line counts.|Different note spelling necessarily.|Different silence durations.|Overtone patterns and sound production affect timbre.
A performance is faster but not louder. Which dimension changed most directly?|Tempo rather than dynamics.|Dynamics rather than tempo.|Pitch rather than tempo.|Staff notation rather than tempo.|Speed and loudness are independent musical dimensions.
Why is a single instrument insufficient to identify an entire culture's music reliably?|Instruments and traditions can overlap and change.|Each instrument belongs to only one culture forever.|Musical contexts never matter.|All music has identical social meaning.|Context and multiple musical features support interpretation.
Two performances use the same notes but different phrasing and dynamics. What conclusion is sound?|Interpretation can change expression without changing the note sequence.|They must be identical in every audible way.|One must use a different alphabet.|Dynamics cannot affect expression.|Performance choices shape musical meaning and character.
## Visual Arts

Which element is two-dimensional and enclosed?|Shape.|Form.|Actual depth.|Mass.|Shape has length and width.
Which element has length, width and depth?|Form.|Flat shape.|Hue.|Contour alone.|Form is three-dimensional.
Value describes what?|Lightness or darkness.|Only color name.|Only surface roughness.|The artwork's price.|Value concerns tonal light and dark.
Texture concerns what?|Actual or suggested surface quality.|Only staff notation.|Only historical date.|Only geometric size.|Texture can be tactile or visually implied.
What is negative space?|The area around or between subjects.|Always black paint.|Only damaged canvas.|Only the frame.|Surrounding spaces have their own shapes.

A distant object is drawn smaller than a similar nearby object. What is suggested?|Depth.|A change in the actual object's size necessarily.|An absence of space.|A new hue alone.|Relative size can create spatial recession.
Which change makes red paint lighter in a conventional tint mixture?|Adding white.|Adding only black.|Adding more of the same red only.|Removing the brush.|White raises value to create a tint.
An artist uses rough marks to depict tree bark. Which element is emphasized?|Texture.|Pitch.|Tempo.|Grammar.|The marks imply surface roughness.
A drawing's empty areas help define a vase silhouette. What is being used?|Negative space.|Only primary colors.|Only actual sculpture weight.|Only lettering.|Surrounding space defines the subject's edges.
Which pair has the strongest value contrast?|White and black.|Two similar mid-grays.|Two nearly identical reds of equal value.|Two matching pale blues.|Black and white span the value range.
A clay vessel can be viewed from all sides. Which description fits?|A three-dimensional form.|A flat line only.|A printed word only.|A color value alone.|The vessel occupies actual volume.
A surface looks rough in a painting but feels smooth. What is this?|Implied texture.|Actual rough texture.|An absence of texture representation.|A musical texture.|The appearance suggests a surface not physically present.
Why distinguish hue from value?|Color identity and lightness are different properties.|Every hue has one unchangeable value.|Value means price only.|Hue measures only canvas size.|A color can retain its hue while changing lightness.
A figure blends into an equally dark background. What change may improve visibility?|Increase the value contrast around the figure.|Make all values identical.|Remove every edge and contrast.|Add an unrelated title only.|Contrast helps separate figure from ground.
Two viewers interpret an image differently. What makes an interpretation more defensible?|Relevant visual details and contextual evidence.|The viewer's volume alone.|The highest purchase price.|A claim with no reference to the work.|Interpretation should be grounded in evidence.
A digital edit changes documentary colors dramatically without disclosure. Why consider both surrounding space and the main subject when analyzing composition?|Their relationship shapes the visual arrangement.|Only the subject can affect appearance.|Empty space has no visible shape.|All backgrounds are identical.|Positive and negative spaces interact.
An artwork uses realistic depth in one area and deliberate flatness elsewhere. ## Physical Education & Sports
What is physical activity?|Bodily movement requiring energy expenditure.|Only competitive sport.|Only watching a match.|Complete rest.|Physical activity includes many forms of movement.

Why warm up progressively?|To prepare the body for activity.|To guarantee no injury can occur.|To replace all skill practice.|To eliminate the need for rest.|A progressive warm-up prepares movement and effort.

Which activity commonly develops aerobic endurance?|Sustained brisk walking.|One brief maximum grip.|One seated stretch only.|Standing motionless.|Sustained movement challenges cardiorespiratory capacity.
What does the frequency component of FITT describe?|How often activity occurs.|How hard it feels.|How long each session lasts.|Which activity is selected.|Frequency counts sessions or occasions.
What does FITT intensity describe?|How hard the activity is.|How many days per week.|The activity's name only.|The session's location.|Intensity concerns effort or workload.
What does FITT time describe?|Duration of activity.|The color of equipment.|Only training days.|The sport's name.|Time refers to session or activity duration.
Which is an example of skill-related balance?|Maintaining body control on one foot.|Memorizing a team roster.|Choosing a uniform color.|Watching a race.|Balance supports stable body positioning.
Why follow rules for equipment spacing?|To reduce collisions and unsafe interference.|To guarantee winning.|To remove every need for awareness.|To make practice longer only.|Spacing helps manage shared activity areas.
A teammate makes a mistake. Which response supports learning?|Offer specific respectful feedback.|Ridicule the person.|Exclude them permanently.|Hide all instructions.|Constructive feedback supports improvement and participation.
What is a suitable response to unusual pain or concerning symptoms during exercise?|Stop and seek appropriate guidance.|Always push harder.|Hide the problem from adults.|Double the workload immediately.|Concerning symptoms require attention rather than forced exertion.
For ages 5–17, WHO recommends on average at least how much moderate-to-vigorous activity daily across the week?|60 minutes.|5 minutes.|15 minutes.|Only one minute.|WHO gives an average daily 60-minute recommendation for this age group.
A runner improves distance but has persistent fatigue. What should be reviewed?|Training load, recovery and appropriate guidance.|Only the shoe color.|How to remove every rest period.|Whether fatigue should always be ignored.|Progress should be evaluated alongside recovery and well-being.
Two pupils need different adaptations for the same activity. A team wins by breaking a safety rule. How should success be evaluated?|Winning does not justify unsafe or dishonest participation.|Winning cancels every rule violation.|Safety applies only to losing teams.|Only the score matters.|Sporting success includes ethical and safe conduct.
Why should an exercise plan consider frequency, intensity, time and type together?|The components jointly determine training demands.|Only one component can ever matter.|More of everything is always better.|Recovery has no relationship to workload.|Training effects depend on the combined plan.
