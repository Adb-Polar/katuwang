## Grammar & Sentence Structure
Choose the verb: Each learner ___ a workbook.|has|have|are having|were having|Each takes a singular verb.
Which is a complete sentence?|The rain stopped.|Although the rain stopped.|After the heavy rain.|Stopping in the rain.|A complete sentence expresses a complete thought.
Identify the adjective in 'The narrow bridge shook.'|narrow|bridge|shook|the|Narrow describes the bridge.
Which word joins clauses to show a reason?|because|but|or|yet|Because introduces a cause or reason.
Which sentence is a question?|Where did Ana go?|Ana went home.|Go home, Ana.|How lovely the garden is!|The sentence asks for information.
Choose the correct past tense of 'bring'.|brought|brang|bringed|bringing|Bring has the irregular past tense brought.
Complete: The box of notebooks ___ heavy.|is|are|were|have been|The subject is the singular noun box.
Choose the sentence with consistent past tense.|We visited the farm and planted seeds.|We visited the farm and plant seeds.|We visit the farm and planted seeds.|We visiting the farm and planted seeds.|Both completed actions use the past tense.
Which revision fixes 'The road flooded we stayed home'?|The road flooded, so we stayed home.|The road flooded, we stayed home.|The road flooded so, we stayed home.|The road, flooded we stayed home.|A comma and coordinating conjunction join the clauses.
Which sentence uses an adverb correctly?|The team worked carefully.|The team worked careful.|The carefully team worked.|The team was carefully.|Carefully modifies the action worked.
Choose the pronoun: Mara and ___ prepared the poster.|I|me|my|mine|A subject position requires I.
Which sentence contains a dependent clause?|We waited until the bus arrived.|The bus arrived at noon.|We waited outside the gate.|The bus arrived, and we boarded.|Until introduces a clause that cannot stand alone here.
Choose the correct possessive for the bags of several students.|the students' bags|the student's bags|the students bags'|the student bags's|A plural noun ending in s takes an apostrophe after s.
Which sentence uses parallel structure?|She enjoys reading, drawing, and dancing.|She enjoys reading, to draw, and dance.|She enjoys to read, drawing, and dance.|She enjoys read, drawing, and to dance.|The three listed activities have the same grammatical form.
Complete: There ___ two folders on the table.|are|is|was|has been|The actual subject two folders is plural.
Which sentence places its modifier clearly?|Carrying an umbrella, Nilo crossed the yard.|Carrying an umbrella, the yard was crossed.|The yard carrying an umbrella was crossed by Nilo.|An umbrella crossed the yard carrying Nilo.|The opening modifier describes the following subject Nilo.
In formal English, why is 'Neither the teachers nor the principal are absent' incorrect?|The nearer subject principal requires is.|Neither always requires a plural verb.|Teachers must be replaced by teacher.|Absent cannot follow a linking verb.|With nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
Which revision removes an ambiguous pronoun from 'Liza told Bea that she had won'?|Liza told Bea, 'You have won.'|Liza told Bea that she won it.|She told Bea that she had won.|Liza told her that she had won.|The quotation explicitly identifies Bea as the winner.
Two independent clauses need a contrast. Which punctuation is correct?|The task was difficult; however, we finished.|The task was difficult, however, we finished.|The task was difficult however; we finished.|The task was difficult; however we, finished.|A semicolon precedes the conjunctive adverb; a comma follows it.
Which revision fixes a misplaced limiting word when only Carlo, among the pupils, solved the puzzle?|Only Carlo solved the puzzle.|Carlo only solved the puzzle.|Carlo solved only the puzzle.|Carlo solved the only puzzle.|Only directly precedes the person being singled out.
## Reading Comprehension
'Read the label before opening the bottle.' What should happen first?|Read the label.|Open the bottle.|Discard the label.|Empty the bottle.|Before establishes the order.
'Mina planted seeds in pots beside her window.' Where were the pots?|Beside her window.|Under her bed.|Behind the school.|Inside a cupboard.|The location is explicitly stated.
'The path was muddy after three days of rain.' What caused the mud?|Several days of rain.|A newly painted fence.|A broken lamp.|Dry afternoon wind.|The sentence connects the mud with the rain.
'A turtle moves slowly, whereas a hare runs quickly.' What pattern is used?|Comparison and contrast.|Chronological instructions.|Question and answer.|Problem and solution.|Whereas signals a difference.
'Please return library books by Friday.' What is the writer's purpose?|To give an instruction.|To describe a character.|To narrate an adventure.|To explain a scientific theory.|The sentence asks readers to perform an action.
'Ana whispered because the baby was asleep.' Why did Ana whisper?|To avoid waking the baby.|To call across the street.|To practice a song.|To announce a game.|The reason is supplied by the because clause.
'Rico saw dark clouds and took an umbrella.' What can reasonably be inferred?|He thought rain was possible.|He knew a typhoon would arrive.|He planned to swim.|He disliked sunny weather.|Clouds support the limited inference that rain was possible.
'A garden reduced the canteen's vegetable costs and supplied fresh food.' Which is the best main idea?|The garden benefited the canteen.|The canteen stopped buying all food.|The garden grew only flowers.|Fresh food always costs nothing.|The main idea includes both stated benefits.
'Solar lamps cost more initially but need no purchased fuel during use.' Which statement preserves the contrast?|Higher initial cost can accompany lower fuel costs.|Solar lamps have no purchase cost.|Solar lamps require daily fuel deliveries.|Every solar lamp lasts forever.|The passage contrasts initial expense with fuel savings.
'Only 12 of the 30 surveyed pupils walked to school.' Which claim is supported?|Fewer than half of those surveyed walked.|Most surveyed pupils walked.|No pupils used transport.|All pupils at the school were surveyed.|Twelve is less than half of thirty.
'The author calls the noisy market lively and welcoming.' What tone is suggested?|Appreciative.|Hostile.|Mournful.|Indifferent.|The positive descriptions express approval.
'The jar was empty. Ben checked the shelf for another.' What does another refer to?|Another jar.|Another shelf.|Another Ben.|Another room.|The missing object sought is a jar.
'First wash the rice, then add water, and finally start the cooker.' Which action comes second?|Add water.|Wash the rice.|Start the cooker.|Serve the rice.|Then identifies the second step.
'Exercise strengthens muscles. It can also improve mood.' What role does also play?|It adds a second benefit.|It rejects the first claim.|It introduces a cause.|It marks the final event.|Also signals additional information.
'Unlike metal spoons, wooden spoons conduct heat poorly.' Which conclusion follows?|The materials transfer heat differently.|Wood conducts heat better than metal.|Metal spoons cannot become hot.|All spoons have identical properties.|The comparison concerns different heat conduction.
'The school added bins, yet litter remained because collection was irregular.' What problem still needs attention?|Reliable waste collection.|The color of classroom walls.|The number of school uniforms.|The length of the flagpole.|The stated cause of remaining litter is irregular collection.
'A notice says a drink is popular; a laboratory report measures its sugar content.' Which source better answers how much sugar it contains?|The laboratory report.|The popularity notice.|Either source solely because it is shorter.|Neither because measurement is opinion.|Measured composition directly addresses the question.
'After tutoring began, scores rose; attendance also improved.' Which conclusion is most cautious?|Several changes occurred, so tutoring alone is not proven to be the cause.|Tutoring certainly caused every score increase.|Attendance cannot affect learning.|The scores must have been fabricated.|Co-occurring changes prevent a simple causal attribution.
'All 15 volunteers liked the event.' Why is 'Everyone in town liked it' unsupported?|The volunteers do not necessarily represent the whole town.|Fifteen people cannot express an opinion.|The event must have been canceled.|A positive opinion is always false.|The conclusion generalizes beyond the sampled group.
'A flood article recommends clearing drains but provides only rainfall totals.' Which extra evidence best tests its recommendation?|Evidence linking drain blockage with local flooding.|A list of popular television shows.|The reporter's favorite season.|The color of the rescue vehicles.|The added evidence tests the proposed cause and remedy.
## Vocabulary Building
Which prefix usually means 'not' in unhappy?|un-|re-|pre-|-ful|Un- negates the adjective happy.
Which word is closest in meaning to abundant?|plentiful|scarce|fragile|brief|Abundant describes a large available amount.
Which is the opposite of temporary?|permanent|recent|portable|ordinary|Permanent contrasts with lasting only a short time.
What does the suffix -less mean in fearless?|Without.|Again.|Before.|Able to.|Fearless means without fear.
Which word names a person who writes?|writer|writing|written|writable|The suffix -er can name a person performing an action.
Which word is a synonym of cautious?|careful|careless|cheerful|curious|Cautious means attentive to possible risks.
'The trail was perilous, with loose rocks above a steep drop.' What does perilous mean?|Dangerous.|Crowded.|Short.|Familiar.|The surrounding hazards establish the meaning.
'The instructions were concise: only two clear sentences.' What does concise mean?|Brief but clear.|Long and repetitive.|Uncertain and vague.|Written in secret code.|The context emphasizes brevity and clarity.
Which word best completes 'Please ___ your source in the report'?|cite|site|sight|side|Cite means acknowledge a source.
Which sentence uses their correctly?|Their classroom is upstairs.|Their going upstairs now.|Put the books over their.|Their is a classroom upstairs.|Their shows possession.
'After months without rain, the soil was parched.' Parched means what?|Very dry.|Recently flooded.|Rich in salt.|Covered in snow.|The lack of rain supplies a context clue.
Which word has the most neutral connotation for an inexpensive bag?|Low-cost.|Cheap-looking.|Shoddy.|Worthless.|Low-cost states price without condemning quality.
'Unlike his talkative sister, Jun was reticent.' Reticent most nearly means what?|Reserved.|Angry.|Forgetful.|Boastful.|The contrast with talkative points to reserved speech.
What does revise mean in 'Revise your draft after feedback'?|Review and improve it.|Publish it unchanged.|Copy it into a different font only.|Delete every paragraph.|Revision changes content or organization to improve the draft.
Which word fits the formal sentence 'The evidence ___ the claim'?|supports|props up, dude|kinda likes|vibes with|Supports fits a formal academic register.
In 'The bank approved the loan,' bank means what?|A financial institution.|The side of a river.|A collection of clouds.|A turning movement.|Loan identifies the financial meaning.
'A critic calls a plan ambitious; another calls it unrealistic.' What differs most?|Their evaluations of its feasibility.|The spelling of the plan's title.|Whether the plan exists.|The language's alphabet.|The adjectives convey contrasting judgments.
A report says 'the medicine may reduce symptoms.' Which revision changes the certainty improperly?|The medicine will eliminate symptoms.|The medicine might lessen symptoms.|The medicine could reduce symptoms.|Symptom reduction is possible with the medicine.|Will eliminate overstates both certainty and effect.
Why is 'He is a snake' usually figurative when discussing dishonesty?|It attributes a negative human quality through comparison.|It classifies a human as a reptile scientifically.|It reports the person's body temperature.|It identifies the person's birthplace.|The animal term conveys a figurative characterization.
'Sensitive' and 'sensible' are confused. Which sentence uses sensible correctly?|Taking water on the long walk was sensible.|The skin was sensible to sunlight.|The microphone was sensible to sound.|She felt sensible after the insult.|Sensible means showing good judgment.
## Essay & Paragraph Writing
What does a topic sentence normally present?|A paragraph's central idea.|Every source in a bibliography.|Only the final recommendation.|The author's complete biography.|It identifies the paragraph's focus.
What is a thesis statement?|The essay's central claim or controlling idea.|A list of all punctuation marks.|A quotation without context.|A paragraph's word count.|The thesis guides the essay's argument.
Which belongs in an essay's conclusion?|A synthesis of its main points.|An unrelated new main argument.|Only a new title.|Unexplained raw survey data.|A conclusion brings the developed discussion together.
What is a draft?|A version prepared for further revision.|A list of final examination scores.|A guarantee that editing is unnecessary.|A bibliography arranged randomly.|A draft remains open to improvement.
Which transition usually signals contrast?|However.|Furthermore.|Similarly.|For example.|However introduces a contrasting idea.
What is coherence in writing?|Ideas connect logically.|Every sentence has the same length.|The text avoids all transitions.|Every paragraph repeats the thesis verbatim.|Coherence concerns understandable logical connections.
Which thesis is most focused for a short essay?|A school garden can improve science learning through regular observation.|Gardens are things.|Everything about education is important.|There are many topics in the world.|The focused thesis specifies a manageable claim.
A paragraph argues for shade trees. Which detail best supports it?|Temperature measurements under trees and in exposed areas.|The writer's favorite shoe brand.|A history of kitchen utensils.|An unrelated sports score.|The measurement addresses the benefit of shade.
Which sentence should be removed from a paragraph about conserving water?|My cousin collects stamps.|Fix leaking taps promptly.|Turn taps off while soaping hands.|Reuse suitable water for plants.|Stamp collecting does not develop the paragraph's focus.
After presenting survey numbers, what should an argument do?|Explain how the numbers support its claim.|Assume numbers explain themselves.|Change to an unrelated topic.|Repeat the entire introduction.|Evidence needs interpretation relevant to the claim.
Which organization suits an essay comparing two transport options?|Discuss each option using the same criteria.|Mix unrelated facts without categories.|Present only one option's advantages.|List events by birth date.|Shared criteria allow a fair comparison.
Which revision removes redundancy?|The result was unexpected.|The final result was unexpectedly unexpected.|The result was an unexpected surprise.|The result unexpectedly came as a surprise.|The concise revision retains the meaning without repetition.
An essay claims uniforms save money. What counterargument is relevant?|Some families face high initial purchase costs.|School libraries need shelves.|Students enjoy holidays.|The weather changes daily.|The counterargument challenges the claimed financial benefit.
Why outline before drafting?|To plan the order and relationship of ideas.|To avoid using evidence.|To eliminate the need for a thesis.|To guarantee agreement from readers.|An outline helps organize the developing argument.
A paragraph jumps from causes to solutions without explanation. What improves coherence?|Add a transition connecting the problem with the proposed response.|Add several unrelated quotations.|Repeat the first sentence five times.|Replace evidence with longer words.|The transition makes the logical relationship explicit.
Which is revision rather than proofreading?|Reordering paragraphs to clarify the argument.|Correcting one spelling error.|Adding a missing period.|Fixing a mistyped capital letter.|Revision addresses content and organization.
An essay concludes that a policy works everywhere after one successful school trial. What revision is strongest?|Limit the claim and discuss the need for trials in other settings.|Replace the school's name with a longer name.|Remove the trial's limitations.|State the same conclusion more loudly.|The evidence supports a narrower conclusion.
Two sources disagree about a policy. What should the writer do?|Compare their evidence and explain the disagreement.|Quote only the shorter source regardless of relevance.|Treat disagreement as proof that both are false.|Hide the conflicting evidence.|Evaluation and comparison strengthen the argument.
A paragraph has accurate evidence but no connection to the thesis. What is needed most?|A relevant analytical link or a different paragraph focus.|More decorative adjectives.|A larger font.|A second unrelated statistic.|Evidence must contribute to the essay's argument.
Which response best addresses a strong counterargument?|Acknowledge its valid point and explain the qualified position supported by evidence.|Attack the person making it.|Pretend it was never raised.|Repeat the thesis without explanation.|A reasoned response engages the objection directly.
## Literature Analysis
Who tells a story?|The narrator.|The publisher.|Every reader.|The printer.|The narrator presents the narrative voice.
What is a story's setting?|Its time and place.|Its list of chapter titles.|Its printing cost.|Its author's signature.|Setting locates events in time and space.
What is conflict in a narrative?|A struggle between opposing forces.|A list of descriptions.|The length of the book.|The presence of a title.|Conflict supplies the central struggle.
What is a theme?|An underlying idea explored by a work.|Only its first sentence.|The writer's legal name.|Its exact page count.|Theme concerns the work's larger meaning.
'The moon was a silver coin.' What device appears?|Metaphor.|Simile.|Onomatopoeia.|Literal measurement.|The sentence directly compares unlike things without like or as.
'The leaves danced in the wind.' What device appears?|Personification.|Historical allusion.|Scientific definition.|Direct dialogue.|Dancing gives leaves a human-like action.
'A character returns a lost wallet although no one is watching.' What trait is best supported?|Honesty.|Jealousy.|Impatience.|Vanity.|The action shows integrity without external reward.
'A story uses I and describes only the speaker's experiences.' What viewpoint is used?|First person.|Third-person omniscient.|Second-person instructions.|An impersonal report.|The narrative I identifies first-person narration.
'A locked gate appears whenever the character feels trapped.' What may the gate symbolize?|Restriction.|Unlimited freedom.|Abundant wealth.|Perfect understanding.|The repeated context connects the gate with confinement.
'A reader knows a guest is an impostor, but the host does not.' What is this?|Dramatic irony.|An exact historical record.|A rhyme scheme.|A direct moral.|The audience knows important information a character lacks.
'A hero must choose between loyalty to a friend and obeying a rule.' What conflict is emphasized?|An internal conflict of values.|A conflict with weather alone.|A conflict between two planets.|A conflict about grammar.|The struggle involves competing personal commitments.
'A brief early mention of a weak bridge becomes important later.' What technique is used?|Foreshadowing.|A bibliography.|An unrelated digression.|A final summary.|An early detail prepares readers for a later development.
'The narrator calls a deserted house bleak and oppressive.' What mood is suggested?|Unease.|Celebration.|Amusement.|Contentment.|The descriptions create an unsettling atmosphere.
'A poem repeats the initial sound in silent silver streets.' What device is used?|Alliteration.|End rhyme.|A numerical pattern.|A footnote.|The nearby words repeat an initial consonant sound.
'A character changes from selfish to generous after a loss.' Which analysis is strongest?|The experience develops the character's values.|The character remains static in every respect.|The setting has no relationship to events.|The narrator must be the author.|The change describes character development.
'A story shows two neighbors cooperating after a dispute.' Which possible theme fits?|Cooperation can overcome division.|All disagreements are permanent.|Wealth prevents every conflict.|Neighbors never influence each other.|The events support reconciliation through cooperation.
An unreliable narrator claims to be calm but describes trembling hands. What should readers examine?|The contradiction between the claim and described behavior.|Only the narrator's claim.|The book's retail price.|Whether all sentences have equal length.|Conflicting evidence may undermine the narrator's self-description.
A character's words express kindness but repeated actions cause deliberate harm. Which evidence should an analysis prioritize?|The pattern of actions together with the contradictory words.|Only the kind words.|Only the chapter numbers.|The cover illustration alone.|Interpreting both types of evidence avoids taking claims at face value.
Two plausible themes are proposed. What best determines which interpretation is stronger?|The consistency and relevance of textual evidence.|Which interpretation is longer.|Which reader speaks first.|The number of adjectives in the interpretation.|A defensible interpretation rests on the text.
A poem never names grief but repeatedly describes absence after a farewell. What is the most defensible analysis?|Grief is a supported interpretation, though it should be argued from details.|The poem proves the author experienced a specific bereavement.|The poem has no meaning without an explicit theme sentence.|Every possible interpretation is equally supported.|Textual inference can support a theme without proving biography.
## Public Speaking & Oral Communication
Who receives a spoken message?|The audience.|Only the microphone.|The speech outline.|The stage curtain.|The audience interprets the message.
What is feedback in communication?|A receiver's response.|The speaker's footwear.|The room's floor plan.|A deleted outline.|Feedback shows how a message is received.
What is an informative speech mainly intended to do?|Explain or clarify a subject.|Force an immediate purchase.|Hide relevant information.|Entertain without conveying information.|Its central purpose is understanding.
What does an introduction usually provide?|A clear opening and topic preview.|Every detail of the conclusion.|Only a bibliography.|An unrelated closing joke.|It orients listeners to the speech.
What is nonverbal communication?|Meaning conveyed through gestures and similar cues.|Only printed citations.|Every spoken noun.|A telephone number.|Gestures and facial expressions can communicate meaning.
What is active listening?|Attending carefully and checking understanding.|Planning a reply while ignoring the speaker.|Interrupting every sentence.|Agreeing without hearing the message.|Active listening involves attention and comprehension.
A speaker addresses younger pupils. Which adjustment is appropriate?|Explain unfamiliar terms using concrete examples.|Use unexplained specialist jargon.|Keep the same dense technical wording.|Read faster to include more terminology.|Audience adaptation supports understanding.
Listeners look confused after a definition. What should the speaker do?|Rephrase it and provide an example.|Repeat it much faster.|Ignore the reaction.|Add unrelated statistics.|Feedback suggests that clarification is needed.
Which is an appropriate visual aid for changes across several months?|A clearly labeled line graph.|A page of tiny unreadable text.|An unrelated portrait.|A table with no dates or headings.|A line graph can show a trend over time.
A persuasive speaker cites a qualified expert accurately. Which appeal is strengthened?|Credibility.|Rhyme.|Volume alone.|Chronology alone.|Relevant expertise supports ethos or credibility.
A student speaks too quickly for listeners to follow. What helps most?|Use deliberate pauses between key points.|Add more slides without pausing.|Raise the pitch continuously.|Remove every explanation.|Pauses give listeners processing time.
Which response best checks understanding of instructions?|Paraphrase the steps and ask whether they are correct.|Say yes without listening.|Repeat only the last word.|Change the task immediately.|Paraphrasing reveals what the listener understood.
During a question period, a speaker does not know an answer. What is ethical?|Acknowledge the limit and offer to verify it.|Invent a confident answer.|Attack the questioner.|Present a guess as established fact.|Honesty maintains trust and avoids misinformation.
A room has loud construction noise. What communication barrier is present?|Environmental noise.|A grammatical tense.|A source citation.|An emotional appeal.|External sound interferes with message reception.
Which conclusion best supports a persuasive speech?|Summarize the reasoned case and give a clear feasible action.|Introduce an unexplained new main claim.|Read the entire speech again.|End with an unrelated statistic.|The ending reinforces the argument and requested action.
A speaker rehearses with a timer. What is being checked?|Whether content fits the allotted duration.|Whether every listener will agree.|Whether the topic needs evidence.|Whether citations can be omitted.|Timed rehearsal tests pacing and length.
A popular speaker makes a medical claim outside their expertise. How should listeners evaluate it?|Seek relevant medical evidence rather than relying on fame.|Accept it because the speaker is famous.|Reject all expert evidence automatically.|Judge only the speaker's speaking speed.|Celebrity is not evidence of subject expertise.
A speaker uses a frightening image unrelated to the claim. What is the strongest criticism?|The emotional appeal does not supply relevant support.|Emotions never affect communication.|Images are always unethical.|Every photograph proves causation.|Persuasion requires evidence relevant to the conclusion.
A team divides a presentation into sections that contradict each other. What should it do before delivery?|Agree on terms and reconcile the conflicting claims.|Use louder microphones.|Add more animation effects.|Make all slides the same color only.|Shared meaning and consistent claims are essential.
A message has accurate words but a sarcastic tone that suggests the opposite. What should a listener consider?|The interaction of verbal and nonverbal cues.|The words alone in every situation.|Only the number of sentences.|Only the speaker's clothing.|Tone can alter how the words are interpreted.
## Research Writing
What is a research question?|A focused question an investigation seeks to answer.|A list of page margins.|An unchangeable personal opinion.|Only a title in bold.|It directs the investigation.
What is a citation?|An acknowledgment identifying a source.|A promise that every claim is correct.|A decorative heading.|A substitute for reading.|It gives credit and helps readers trace evidence.
What is plagiarism?|Presenting another person's work as one's own.|Quoting with proper attribution.|Collecting data with consent.|Summarizing with a source citation.|Plagiarism conceals intellectual borrowing.
What is a primary source for a student's survey study?|The student's collected survey responses.|A textbook summary of surveys.|An encyclopedia article about polls.|A classmate's opinion of the topic.|The original responses are direct research data.
What does a bibliography or reference list identify?|The sources used or cited.|Only the writer's classmates.|Every possible source ever published.|Only the document's word count.|It records source information for readers.
What is a paraphrase?|A restatement in one's own wording with acknowledgment.|A copied sentence without quotation marks.|A change of font only.|A list of unexplained numbers.|Paraphrasing retains meaning while changing expression and citing the source.
Which research question is most manageable?|How do 30 Grade 8 pupils travel to school this month?|Why does everything happen?|What is all knowledge?|How can every world problem be solved?|It specifies a feasible population, subject and period.
A report uses an undated health claim. What should be checked?|Its currency and supporting medical evidence.|Only the page's background color.|Whether its title is exciting.|The number of advertisements.|Currency and evidence affect reliability.
Which source best supports a claim about a law's wording?|The official text of the law.|An anonymous meme.|A friend's recollection.|A commercial slogan.|The primary legal text directly establishes wording.
A quotation is shortened with an ellipsis. What must remain intact?|The source's intended meaning.|Every original page break.|The source's font size.|Only the quotation's first word.|Omission must not distort meaning.
Why describe the sampling method?|So readers can judge who the findings represent.|To make the report look longer.|To hide the number of respondents.|To avoid explaining limitations.|Sampling determines the scope of defensible inference.
A study surveys only basketball players about school sports. Which limitation matters?|Other pupils' preferences may be underrepresented.|No athlete can answer a survey.|The study automatically represents all pupils.|Sports preferences cannot be studied.|The sample excludes relevant groups.
Where should a methods section explain data collection?|In a clear account of procedures and participants.|Only in the title.|In an unrelated quotation.|Nowhere if the findings look plausible.|Transparent methods allow evaluation and replication.
A graph uses percentages from 40 respondents. What should its caption report?|The sample size and what the percentages measure.|Only a decorative title.|An unrelated population total.|A guarantee of universal accuracy.|Readers need context to interpret percentages.
A source sells the product it praises. What should a researcher examine?|Potential conflicts of interest and independent evidence.|Only whether its logo is attractive.|Whether the article is short.|Only its positive adjectives.|Financial interests can influence presentation of evidence.
What should a researcher do with findings that challenge the hypothesis?|Report and examine them honestly.|Delete them solely because they disagree.|Change responses without disclosure.|Claim the hypothesis was proven anyway.|Research integrity requires reporting contrary evidence.
Two websites repeat the same unsourced claim. Does this establish independent confirmation?|No; both may derive from one unverified source.|Yes; repetition always verifies a claim.|Yes; two pages equal two experiments.|No; websites can never contain evidence.|Independence and provenance matter more than repetition.
A study finds a correlation between screen time and sleep. Which wording is justified without causal evidence?|Screen time was associated with sleep duration in the sample.|Screen time certainly caused every sleep problem.|Sleep can never affect screen use.|The relationship proves a treatment works.|Correlation alone does not establish causation.
A researcher changes the hypothesis after seeing results. What preserves transparency?|Describe the change and label the new analysis exploratory.|Pretend it was specified beforehand.|Alter the original data to match it.|Omit the methods section.|Disclosure distinguishes exploration from prespecified testing.
Which revision best limits an overgeneralized conclusion from one class?|The result describes this class and warrants testing in other groups.|The result proves all learners behave identically.|The class represents every country automatically.|No conclusion of any kind is possible.|A qualified conclusion matches the evidence's scope.
